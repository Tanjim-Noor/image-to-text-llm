# The remaining pillars of faith

In Levels 1 and 2, we covered the first of the pillars of faith comprehensively, namely belief in Allah ﷺ, the belief that He Alone is to be worshipped, and belief in His Lordship and in His names and attributes. In this level, we will discuss the remaining pillars of faith.

## The second pillar: Belief in the angels

What is meant by the angels

The Arabic word mala’ikah (angels) is the plural of malak, which is derived from the word alukah, meaning message. The related word mal’ak (or malak) refers to an angel, because he conveys the message from Allah ﷺ. The verb alaka means “he carried the message.”

At-Tabari ﷺ said: The angels are called al-mala’ikah because they carry messages; they are messengers of Allah between Him and His prophets and those among His slaves to whom they are sent.

Or the word may be derived from al-malk which refers to seizing with power.

In Islamic terminology, the mala’ikah are among the beings created by Allah ﷺ. Allah ﷺ created them from light, and they are controlled and subjugated by Him. They are honoured slaves of Allah who never disobey Allah’s commands and do what they are commanded to do. They cannot be described as male or female; they do not eat or drink, they do not become weary or tired, and they do not get married. No one knows their number except Allah.

Some scholars have described them as being entities of light who have been given the ability to change shape and appear in different forms, by Allah’s leave.

# The importance of believing in the angels:

Believing in the angels is the second of the pillars of faith, and a person’s faith is not valid unless he affirms that, believes in the existence of the angels and believes what has been narrated in the Quran and Sunnah about their attributes and deeds.

Allah ﷺ says: *[The Messenger has believed in what was revealed to him from his Lord, and [so have] the believers. All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers]* [al-Baqarah 2:285], *[Whosoever is an enemy to Allah and His angels and His messengers and Gabriel and Michael – then indeed, Allah is an enemy to the disbelievers]* [al-Baqarah 2:98].

## Belief in the angels includes four things

1. Believing that they exist in a real sense.

2. Believing in those whose names we know, such as Jibreel; as for those whose names we do not know, we believe in them in general terms.

3. Believing in what we know of their attributes, such as the attributes of Jibreel. The Prophet ﷺ told us that he saw him in his form in which he was created, with six hundred wings, and he filled the horizon. 

An angel may change shape, by Allah’s command, and turn into human form, as happened with Jibreel when Allah ﷻ sent him to Maryam, and he appeared before her in the form of a perfect human being.

4. Believing in what we know of their deeds, which they undertake by the command of Allah, such as glorifying Him and worshipping Him night and day, without growing weary or tired.

# The third pillar: Belief in the Books

In Arabic, the word **kitab (book)** refers to anything that is written and put together. The Quran is called a **kitab (book)** because of what is compiled in it of stories, likenesses, beliefs, commands, prohibitions and legislation, or because it includes all of the previous Books.

What is meant by the **Books** here is the books and scriptures that contained the words of Allah ﷻ, that He revealed to His messengers ﷺ.

## The importance of believing in the Books

Believing in the Books is one of the basic principle of the Islamic creed aqidah and one of the pillars of faith. No one’s faith is valid unless he believes in the Books that Allah sent down to His messengers ﷺ. Allah ﷻ says: *(O you who have believed, believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Book that He sent down upon His Messenger and the Scripture which He sent down before. And whosoever disbelieves in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, and the Last Day has certainly gone far astray)* [an-Nisa 4:136].

## The books that Allah ﷺ sent down include the following:

**The Torah:** this is the Book of Allah which was brought by Musa ﷺ. Allah ﷻ says: *(And We gave Moses the Scripture, after We had destroyed the former generations, as enlightenment for the people)* [al-Qasas 28:43].

According to the hadith about the debate between Adam and Musa ﷺ, which was narrated from Abu Hurayrah ﷺ, from the Prophet ﷺ: “Adam said to him: O Musa, Allah chose you to speak to directly, He wrote the Torah for you with His own Hand.” Narrated in as-Sahihayn.

# The fourth pillar: Belief in the messengers

What is meant by the messengers

In linguistic terms, the word rasool (messenger) is derived from irsal, which means sending.
In Islamic terminology, it refers to someone whom Allah chose to receive His revelation and convey it to a disbelieving people.
It was also said that it refers to a person who was sent to people who had drifted away, to remind them of the command to believe in Allah Alone (Tawhid).

## Definition of the word nabiy (prophet)

In linguistic terms, the word nabiy (prophet) is derived from naba, which means news.
Allah ﷻ says:  ﴾About what are they asking one another? About the great news (an-naba’ il-‘azeem) [an-Naba 78:1-2].
A prophet is called nabiy because he is informed of something and informs others of it.
In Islamic terminology, the word nabiy refers to someone whom Allah chose to receive His revelation and commanded him to act in accordance with it.

The difference between a messenger (rasool) and a prophet (nabiy):

Some of the scholars are of the view that there is no difference between a messenger and a prophet, but this is not correct.
Some of them are of the view that there is a difference between them, as they said: The messenger is the one to whom a law was revealed, and he was commanded to convey it.
And the prophet is the one to whom revelation was sent, but he was not commanded to convey it.

This is far-fetched for several reasons:

➡️ Firstly, Allah stated that He sent the prophets as He sent the messengers. He ﷻ says: ﴾And We did not send before you any messenger or prophet except that…[al-Hajj 22:52]. Being sent implies that the prophet was to convey the message.

🚫 Secondly: not conveying means concealing the revelation of Allah ﷻ, and Allah did not send down His revelation for it to be concealed and withheld in the heart of any person, so that this knowledge would die when that person died.

UNIT 2: Belief in the messengers 21

# Thirdly: the Messenger ﷺ said: “The nations were shown to me, and a prophet came by accompanied by one man, a prophet accompanied by two men, a prophet accompanied by a small group, and a prophet who was not accompanied by anyone.” Agreed upon.

This indicates that the Prophets conveyed the religion of Allah ﷻ, and that they varied in terms of the response to their call.

It was said with regard to the difference between them that the rasool (messenger) is one to whom a new law was revealed, and the nabiy (prophet) is one who was sent to confirm the law of the one who came before him.

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah said: ‘The rasool is the one who was sent to a disbelieving people who rejected the truth, and the nabiy is the one who was sent to a believing people with the law of the rasool who came before him, to teach them and judge between them.

Perhaps this is the view that is most likely to be correct.

## The importance of believing in the messengers:

Belief in the messengers is one of the basic principles of faith, without which a Muslim’s faith is not complete. Whoever disbelieves in one of them has disbelieved in Allah ﷻ and in all the messengers ﷺ.

Allah ﷻ says: *Indeed, those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and wish to make a distinction between [belief in] Allah and [belief in] His messengers and say, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others,” and wish to adopt a way in between, Those are the disbelievers, truly. And We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment. But they who believe in Allah and His messengers and do not discriminate between any of them - to those He is going to give their rewards. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful!* [An-Nisa 4:150-152].

## What is meant by believing in the messengers:

Believing in the Messengers means believing firmly that Allah ﷻ sent a messenger to every nation, to call them to worship Allah Alone and to reject everything that is worshipped besides Him; and that they were all sent and spoke the truth, and they conveyed everything with which Allah sent them.

# The fifth pillar: Belief in the Last Day

The Last Day is the Day of Resurrection, on which people will be resurrected for the reckoning and the requital.

It is so called because there will be no day after it, as the people of Paradise will settle in their abodes, and the people of Hell will settle in their abodes.

## What is meant by believing in the Last Day:

Believing firmly that this Day will come to pass. So it means believing that Allah ﷻ will resurrect the people from their graves, then He will call them to account and will requite them for their deeds, until the people of Paradise settle in their abodes and the people of Hell settle in their abodes.

The Last Day is also called: al-waqi’ah (the inevitable occurrence), al-haqqah (the inevitable Hour), al-qari’ah (the striking calamity), ar-rajfah (the earthquake), as-saakhkhah (the deafening blast), al-faz’ al-akbar (the greatest terror), Yawm al-Hisab (the day of reckoning) and Yawm ad-Deen (the day of judgement).

Believing in the Last Day includes believing in everything that will happen after death, such as:

1.  Punishment and bliss in the grave for both body and soul together, and the trial of the grave, which is the questioning of the deceased, after his burial, about his Lord, his religion and his Prophet.

2.  The resurrection and the gathering, which is when the dead will be brought back to life and brought forth from their graves, and their souls will be restored to their bodies, then all people will stand before the Lord of the Worlds. Then they will be gathered in one place. Allah ﷻ says: *{Then indeed, after that you are to die. Then indeed you, on the Day of Resurrection, will be resurrected}* [al-Mu’minun 23:15-16].

*   In linguistic terms, the word qada refers to doing something well and finishing it properly.

*   In linguistic terms, the word qadar refers to evaluating and knowing how much a thing is; in other words, becoming aware of its size or estimate. This refers to awareness of the extent of things.

UNIT 3: Belief in the Last Day and in the divine will and decree 29

# Al-qada wa’l-qadar in Islamic terminology:

Some of the scholars are of the view that these two words mean the same, namely Allah’s decreeing how all things are to be, in accordance with His prior knowledge and as dictated by His wisdom.

Other scholars of the view that there is a difference between them, so al-qadar refers to the general, holistic decree from eternity,

And al-qada refers to the details of that decree and how it will unfold.

Thus Allah ﷺ decrees [qadar] that a particular thing should happen at its appointed time, then when the time comes at which it is decreed that this thing should happen, it comes to pass [qada].

This view is the most correct view.

## Ruling on believing in the divine will and decree:

Belief in the divine will and decree is one of the six pillars of faith. That is supported by the Quran and Sunnah, and by scholarly consensus. Whoever rejects belief in the divine will and decree has disbelieved in Allah ﷺ and has gone beyond the bounds of Islam. Allah ﷺ says: *[And ever is the command of Allah a destiny decreed] (al-Ahzab 33:38), [But it was so that Allah might accomplish a matter already destined] (al-Anfal 8:42), [Indeed, all things We created with predestination] (al-Qamar 54:49).*

In the hadith of Jibreel ﷺ that is narrated in Sahih Muslim it says: «… And to believe in the divine decree, both good and bad.» And the Prophet ﷺ said: «You should understand that whatever has befallen you could not have missed you, and whatever has missed you could not have befallen you.» *Narrated by Ahmad and Abu Dawud, classified as sahih by al-Albani.*

During the Jahiliyyah, the Arabs were aware of the divine decree and never denied it. The poet Antarah said:

O Allah how could I escape my death, if my Lord has already decreed it from heaven?

One of the scholars of Arabic language said: I know of no Arab who denied the divine will and decree. And he said: All the Arabs, both before and after the advent of Islam, affirmed the concept of the divine decree, both good and bad. Then he said in verse:

Even the stitching of a needle is subject to the divine decree;
A needle will not stop stitching except in accordance with the divine decree.

## Definition of kufr:

In linguistic terms, the word kufr (translated above as disbelief) means covering and concealing. So the verb kafara (cover) may be used to refer to the night, because it conceals everything with its darkness; and it may be used to refer to the sea, because it covers everything that is in it; and it may be used to refer to the clouds, because they cover the sun. 
From the same root comes the word kafaarah (expiation), because kafaarah covers sins, such as kafaarat al-yamin (expiation for breaking an oath) and kafaarat az-zihaar (expiation for zihaar – a jahili form of divorce in which a man says to his wife, “You are to me as my mother’s back”).

In Islamic terminology, **kufr (disbelief)** refers to not believing in Allah and His messengers, whether or not that is accompanied by rejection. It refers to any doubt or turning away from belief, out of envy or arrogance, or following whims and desires that distract one from following the message. 
The connection between the linguistic meaning and the religious meaning is that the kafir (disbeliever) has covered his heart so that faith could not enter it. Al-Layth said: “The kafir is only called thus because kufr has covered his heart.”

# The Zad Academy Series:
